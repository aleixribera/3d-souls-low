How theOnline Works

A short, plain guide to how your game, Aleix's home server and the internet work together, so a fight with friends feels fair.

Begin
I

The Home Server

One computer in Aleix's home runs the game around the clock. It's the referee everyone connects to.

Your game → server: "here's what I did" Server → everyone: "here's what the world is doing"
60/ SEC

The server plays the fight 60 times a second. It has no graphics; it only keeps score of what's happening.

16PLAYERS

That's how many people one lobby holds by default. The leader can add up to 3 computer-controlled bots to help.

1LINK

Friends just open a web address. They don't install anything or change their router.

II

Packets: Tiny Notes

Your game and the server never stop passing small notes to each other, around thirty every second in each direction.

Your gameServer

Only what changed

The first time you join, the server sends you the whole picture. After that it only sends what changed. If a door hasn't moved, nobody mentions the door. Bigger notes are also compressed, like zipping a file.

Measuring the delay

Every 2 seconds your game sends a quick "ping" and times how long the reply takes. That round trip is your ping, and it's the number people mean when they talk about lag.

On a good connection, a note usually gets there and back in a few hundredths of a second. That's faster than a blink.

III

Who Decides What

Nothing is decided in two places. Each thing in the game has exactly one owner.

Your game decides about you

so your controls feel instant
  • Where you move and which way you face
  • Whether your roll dodged
  • Whether you blocked or parried
  • Your health, stamina and flasks

The server decides about the world

so everyone sees the same fight
  • Every enemy and boss: where it is and what attack it's doing
  • How much health the boss has left
  • Doors, traps and boss phases
  • The damage scoreboard at the end

The Leader

One player is the leader. That's the person who most recently touched their controls. They choose the adventure, start the run and add bots. Being connected isn't enough: after five minutes without pressing anything, the leader title passes to someone who's actually playing.

V

When Screens Disagree

Because of lag, your screen and the server are never showing exactly the same moment, so something has to settle it.

Whatever happens to you
is decided on your screen.
That's the one rule the whole system is built around.
Many online games
You roll→The server says "too late"→You get hit anyway
This game
You roll→Your screen: dodged→The server accepts it

The boss attacks you

  • Your game checks the hit against the boss as you see it.
  • If you rolled through it on your screen, you dodged.
  • No late message ever changes that to "actually, you got hit."

You parry

  • If you see the parry land, it landed, and the attack stops right away.
  • Your game tells the server exactly which attack you parried.
  • A parry that arrives late can't cancel a different, newer attack by mistake.

You hit the boss

  • Your game reports the hit to the server.
  • The server works out the damage from the game's own numbers.
  • Everyone shares one boss health bar, so you all see the same thing.

Executions

  • If two players go for the same kneeling boss, the server gives it to just one of them.
  • The boss can't die or change phase in the middle of that player's animation.
VI

Why It Never Feels Like Cheating

Three promises the game keeps, even when the connection is bad.

THE BIG ONE

No hits you dodged

In many online games the server "corrects" you afterwards, and that's what feels like cheating. Here, if you dodged on your screen, you dodged.

You always see it coming

Even on a slow connection, a boss attack is never shown with its warning skipped. You always get the wind-up, so you always have time to react.

Instant controls

Moving, rolling and blocking never wait for the internet. Your character responds the moment you press a button.

The trade-off

The game trusts each player about their own dodges. That's fine for friends playing together, but it isn't built to stop cheaters. The point is to make co-op fair and responsive, not to run a competitive match.

VII

Leaving and Coming Back

The world doesn't stop when you close the tab.

01

The run keeps going

The server keeps the adventure alive even if everyone leaves. The bots stay, and the enemies go back to where they started.

02

It remembers you

Your browser keeps a secret key. When you come back, the server recognises you and gives you back your health and flasks.

03

Nobody waits forever

When a fight is loading, players get 30 seconds to be ready. After that the others start without them.

You control yourself.
The home server controls the world.

They pass about thirty small notes a second to stay in sync.
